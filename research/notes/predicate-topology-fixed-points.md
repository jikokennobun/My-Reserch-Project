## Working Summary

This appears to connect APS to topological or domain-theoretic structure: predicates, opens, filters, continuity, and fixed points.

## Detailed Reconstruction

The Project direction is to replace purely syntactic diagonalization by
topological or domain-theoretic fixed-point principles. In ordinary APS, a
Jeroslow-style fixed point

$$
p\simeq\boxtimes p
$$

is supplied by syntax. In an analytic APS, one instead asks whether the space of
predicates carries enough topology or order-completeness for fixed points to
exist by continuity or compactness.

## Predicate Space

Let $L$ be an APS preorder. A predicate topology can be introduced by taking
basic opens of the form:

$$
U_a=\{x\in L:a\le x\},
$$

or dually:

$$
V_a=\{x\in L:x\le a\}.
$$

The choice matters. If order is read as information growth, upper sets are
Scott-open candidates; if order is read as proof strength, lower sets may be
more natural for refutability. This is one reason the topology must be fixed
explicitly rather than treated as decoration.

## Domain-Theoretic Fixed Points

Suppose $(D,\sqsubseteq)$ is a dcpo with bottom $\bot_D$ and

$$
F:D\to D
$$

is Scott-continuous. Then the Kleene fixed-point theorem gives:

$$
\mu F=\bigsqcup_{n<\omega}F^n(\bot_D).
$$

For APS, one wants to apply this to operators generated by $\Box$,
$\boxtimes$, implication, or negation-like transformations. Since $\boxtimes$
is antitone in A1, a direct monotone fixed-point theorem may not apply. Possible
remedies are:

- study $\boxtimes^2$, which is monotone when $\boxtimes$ is antitone;
- pass to an order-dual domain;
- use bilattice or polarity semantics;
- treat fixed points of $\boxtimes$ as two-cycle collapse points.

## Continuity Conditions

A topological or domain-theoretic APS should specify whether:

$$
\Box\left(\bigvee_i x_i\right)=\bigvee_i\Box x_i,
$$

or

$$
\boxtimes\left(\bigvee_i x_i\right)=\bigwedge_i\boxtimes x_i
$$

for directed joins. The second equation is contravariant and must be handled
with care.

## Analytic APS Program

The analytic program can be stated as:

1. choose a topology/order-completion on $L$;
2. extend $\Box$ and $\boxtimes$ continuously or semicontinuously;
3. obtain fixed points by domain/topological arguments;
4. prove when those fixed points are principal, compact, or definable;
5. feed the reflected fixed points into the APS G2/FG2 theorem.

This connects directly to the completion-reflection problem:

$$
L\to\widehat L.
$$

A completion may create non-syntactic fixed points. The research question is
which topological hypotheses force such fixed points to come from actual
formulas in $L$.
