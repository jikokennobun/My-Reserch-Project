## Abstract

The G2-ZOO currently contains many finite preAPS witnesses showing that G2,
FG2, syntactic fixed points, completion fixed points, and residuation can split
apart. This note asks which of those splittings survive once the underlying
preorder is equipped with a topology or domain structure compatible with
provability and refutability. The aim is not to add decoration to APS, but to
identify continuity, compactness, and reflection hypotheses that force the
chaotic finite examples to approximate syntactic fixed-point behavior.

The central problem is:

$$
\text{Which topological APS hypotheses imply, separate, or refute G2/FG2?}
$$

The expected answer is a taming spectrum. At one end, discrete finite topology
adds no mathematical strength. At the other end, algebraic dcpo structure plus
Scott-continuity and compact reflection should force completion-generated fixed
points to have syntactic content.

## Background

An APS or preAPS is written:

$$
S=(L,\le,\Box,\boxtimes,T,\bot).
$$

The two incompleteness principles tracked throughout the repository are:

$$
\mathrm{G2}(S):
\quad
\boxtimes T\le\bot\Rightarrow T\le\bot,
$$

and:

$$
\mathrm{FG2}(S):
\quad
\boxtimes\boxtimes T\le\boxtimes T.
$$

The $n$-FG2 hierarchy is:

$$
\mathrm{nFG2}(k):
\quad
\boxtimes^{k+1}T\le\boxtimes^kT
\qquad(k\ge 1).
$$

The topological problem is meaningful because the finite ZOO already proves
that order-only hypotheses are weak. If topology merely makes every finite
carrier discrete, no separation result changes. A useful topological APS must
therefore constrain either:

1. which finite models are admissible as approximants;
2. which infinite limits are allowed;
3. how $\boxtimes$ extends to a completion;
4. how completion fixed points reflect back to formulas.

## Candidate Topological APS

A topological preAPS is a preAPS together with a topology $\tau$ on $L$:

$$
(L,\tau,\le,\Box,\boxtimes,T,\bot).
$$

The topology is not arbitrary. The following compatibility conditions are the
main candidates.

### Order Compatibility

The specialization preorder of $\tau$ should refine or coincide with $\le$:

$$
x\le_\tau y
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
x\le y,
$$

or preferably:

$$
x\le_\tau y
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x\le y.
$$

For a poset this suggests the Alexandrov topology, the Scott topology, or a
spectral topology generated by compact approximants.

### Provability Continuity

The provability operator should preserve limits of directed approximation:

$$
\Box\left(\bigvee_i x_i\right)
=
\bigvee_i \Box x_i
$$

whenever the directed join exists. Equivalently, for a dcpo $L$, require
$\Box:L\to L$ to be Scott-continuous.

### Refutability Continuity

Since $\boxtimes$ is usually antitone, it should be treated as a monotone map
into the order dual:

$$
\boxtimes:L\to L^{op}.
$$

The continuity requirement is then Scott-continuity of this dual-valued map:

$$
\boxtimes\left(\bigvee_i x_i\right)
=_{L^{op}}
\bigvee_i^{L^{op}}\boxtimes x_i,
$$

which is equivalently:

$$
\boxtimes\left(\bigvee_i x_i\right)
=
\bigwedge_i \boxtimes x_i
$$

when the displayed meet exists in $L$.

### Compact Reflection

Let $K(L)$ be the compact elements of an algebraic dcpo. A completion fixed
point is useful only if it can be approximated by, or reflected to, compact
syntax-like elements. The key reflection condition is:

$$
q=\widehat{\boxtimes}q
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\exists k\in K(L)\ (k\le q\ \text{and}\ k\approx\boxtimes k)
$$

for an explicitly chosen equivalence or approximation relation $\approx$.

The strict version asks for an actual syntactic fixed point:

$$
\exists p\in L\quad p=\boxtimes p.
$$

## Taming Axes

The topology should be classified by how much of the G2-ZOO it eliminates.

### Axis T1: Trivial Topology

Every finite model is given the discrete topology. Then all maps are continuous,
so the topology adds no restriction. This axis is useful only as a negative
control.

### Axis T2: Alexandrov Order Topology

Open sets are upper sets:

$$
U\in\tau
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x\in U,\ x\le y\Rightarrow y\in U.
$$

Continuity of a map between Alexandrov spaces is exactly monotonicity. Thus
$\Box$-continuity corresponds to monotonicity, while $\boxtimes$-continuity
must be read against the dual topology.

This level mostly repackages A1-style monotonicity and antitonicity. It may
filter out examples with badly behaved $\boxtimes$, but it should not by itself
force FG2.

### Axis T3: Scott Domain Structure

Assume $L$ is a dcpo and $\Box$ and $\boxtimes:L\to L^{op}$ are
Scott-continuous. Now fixed-point theorems become available, but usually for
monotone maps. The natural monotone composite is:

$$
\boxtimes^2:L\to L.
$$

If $\boxtimes^2$ is Scott-continuous and has a prefixed or postfixed point
condition, then domain-theoretic fixed-point methods can be applied to the
even refutability dynamics.

### Axis T4: Algebraic or Spectral APS

Assume $L$ is algebraic and compact elements represent syntactic formulas,
finite theories, or finite proof states. The topology is then required to make
semantic fixed points visible at compact stages. This is the first level at
which completion fixed points can plausibly imply syntactic information.

## Basic Observations

### Proposition 1: Discrete Finite Topology Is Vacuous

Let $S$ be a finite preAPS. If $L$ is given the discrete topology, then every
function $L\to L$ is continuous. Consequently, adding the discrete topology
does not change the G2, FG2, nFG2, fixed-point, or residuation profile of $S$.

Proof. In a discrete space every subset is open, and preimages of open sets are
open under every function. The APS order and the orbit

$$
T,\boxtimes T,\boxtimes^2T,\ldots
$$

are unchanged. Therefore the truth values of all order-defined principles are
unchanged.

This proposition is a warning: any nontrivial taming theorem must use a topology
canonically induced by order, syntax, approximation, or proof structure.

### Proposition 2: Alexandrov Continuity Recovers Order Monotonicity

Let $L$ and $M$ be posets with the upper Alexandrov topology. A map
$f:L\to M$ is continuous iff it is monotone.

For $\boxtimes$ this should be applied to:

$$
\boxtimes:L\to L^{op}.
$$

Thus $\boxtimes$ is continuous in the Alexandrov sense iff it is antitone as a
map $L\to L$.

Proof sketch. Upper sets in $M$ pull back to upper sets in $L$ exactly when
$x\le y$ implies $f(x)\le f(y)$. Replacing $M$ by $L^{op}$ converts monotonicity
there into antitonicity in $L$.

### Proposition 3: Even Refutability Dynamics Is Monotone

If $\boxtimes:L\to L$ is antitone, then:

$$
\boxtimes^2:L\to L
$$

is monotone. Therefore fixed-point methods for monotone maps apply to
$\boxtimes^2$, even when they do not apply directly to $\boxtimes$.

This matters because FG2 is precisely the first comparison between the first
two nontrivial points in the $\boxtimes$-orbit:

$$
\boxtimes^2T\le\boxtimes T.
$$

Domain-theoretic control of $\boxtimes^2$ is therefore a natural route to
explaining when FG2 stabilizes.

## Topological Taming Conjectures

### Conjecture A: Compact-Reflection Taming

Let $L$ be an algebraic dcpo whose compact elements form a syntax-like
substructure $K(L)$. Suppose:

1. $\Box:L\to L$ is Scott-continuous;
2. $\boxtimes:L\to L^{op}$ is Scott-continuous;
3. $\widehat{\boxtimes}$ has a completion fixed point $q$;
4. every such $q$ is compactly reflected.

Then the completion fixed point yields either a genuine syntactic fixed point:

$$
\exists p\in K(L)\quad p=\boxtimes p,
$$

or a controlled approximation sequence:

$$
p_0,p_1,p_2,\ldots\in K(L),
\qquad
p_{n+1}\le\boxtimes p_n,
$$

whose limit is $q$.

### Conjecture B: Topological nFG2 Stabilization

Assume the $T$-orbit of $\boxtimes$ has a limit $p$ in a topology compatible
with $\le$:

$$
p=\lim_n \boxtimes^nT.
$$

If the tail orbit is eventually order-convergent from above:

$$
\exists N\ \forall n\ge N\quad
\boxtimes^{n+1}T\le\boxtimes^nT,
$$

then all sufficiently high nFG2 principles hold. If the convergence is compactly
reflected, then a finite-stage nFG2 theorem should be extractable.

### Conjecture C: No Taming Without Reflection

Continuity alone should not imply FG2. There should be continuous antitone maps
on non-algebraic or non-reflective domains whose $\boxtimes$-orbit has a limit
but no syntactic fixed point and no finite FG2 stabilization.

This conjecture separates the topological question from a purely analytic
fixed-point theorem: the APS program needs reflection back to syntax, not only
existence of semantic limits.
