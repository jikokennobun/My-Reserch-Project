### Abstract (source metadata)

We study algebras for the $\mathbf{BM}$-, $\mathbf{BI}$-, and $\mathbf{BMI}$-fragments of combinatory logic, where $\mathbf{B} xyz \to_{w} x\left(yz\right)$, $\mathbf{M} x \to_{w} xx$ and $\mathbf{I} x \to_{w} x$. We prove that every finite $\mathbf{BM}$-algebra $\mathcal{A}$ contains an element $Y$ satisfying $Yx = x\left(Yx\right)$ for all $x\in A$. Moreover, a single closed $\mathbf{BM}$-term represents such an element in every finite $\mathbf{BM}$-algebra. On the other hand, we show that there exists an infinite $\mathbf{BMI}$-algebra with no such element. Consequently, the variety of $\mathbf{BM}$-algebras is not generated by finite $\mathbf{BM}$-algebras. We also characterise finite $\mathbf{BI}$-algebras as finite semigroups with two distinguished left units $B$ and $I$, and finite $\mathbf{BMI}$-algebras as finite idempotent semigroups with three distinguished left units $B$, $M$ and $I$.
