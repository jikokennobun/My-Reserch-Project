### Abstract (source metadata)

First introduced for proving Craig's interpolation theorem, Maehara's method provides a constructive, proof-theoretic procedure for extracting interpolants in sequent calculus proofs. Building on this method, Saurin developed a ``proof-relevant'' interpolation theorem for first-order linear logic, in the sense that the interpolated proof is obtained by cut-introduction steps that can be reversed via cut-elimination. In this work, we extend Saurin's proof-relevant interpolation to linear logic proofs with cuts as well as to circular proofs in linear logic with fixed-points. We specifically show how Maehara's method can be extended in a proof-relevant way to work on non-cut-free and non-well-founded (but regular) proof objects, and guarantee that the interpolation procedure preserves strong validity. We also study conditions under which one can interpolate system L terms that are not necessarily in normal form.
